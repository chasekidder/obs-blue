# Phase 2: microphone recording and stream metadata (local only)

Date: 2026-11-24
Branch: `improve/2-recording` (base: `improve/1-foundation`)
Switch: `[recording] enabled` (default `false`)

## Goal

Keep a record of every live that does not depend on OBS or Facebook: the microphone audio as small clips, and the times needed to line them up with the comments and with Facebook's replay. Everything stays on the PC; phase 3 copies it off.

## Behavior with the switch on

- **At Go Live**, once Facebook confirms the live (state `LIVE`), stream-pilot starts `stream-pilot-recorder@<stream dir>.service`, a user unit of its own. It records `[audio] mic_device` straight from PipeWire (PulseAudio protocol) with ffmpeg: Ogg Opus, mono, 48 kHz, `bitrate_kbps` (32), clips of `clip_s` (30 s) in `<stream_dir>/audio/`.
- **Manifest** `audio/manifest.jsonl`, appended and fsynced:
  - a `session` line with `started_at`, the wall-clock time of the session's first sample (the PulseAudio input's start timestamp as ffmpeg reports it; `anchor: "process_start"` if ffmpeg reported none);
  - one `clip` line per finished clip with `start_sample` / `end_sample` (48 kHz samples from the session's first sample: each clip starts where the previous one ended, and its length is the file's exact sample count, its last Ogg granule position minus the Opus pre-skip; ffmpeg's segment times carry the PulseAudio timestamps' jitter of a few samples, found in an 88-minute test) and `start_at`;
  - `gap` lines (`from`, `to`) between sessions, and `session_end` lines.
  Only clips listed in the manifest are finished: ffmpeg lists a clip once it has closed it, so a clip cut short by a crash or power cut is never listed.
- **Crash and resume.** The recorder is not part of `stream-pilot.service`, so a stream-pilot crash or restart does not touch it. When stream-pilot resumes a live and the recorder's unit is not active, it starts it again; the recorder opens a new session and lists the gap. If ffmpeg itself exits (for example the microphone was unplugged), the recorder starts a new session after 5 s, also with a gap.
- **At the end** (power button, long press, `stream-pilot press --long`), after OBS has stopped streaming and before the comment capture stops, stream-pilot stops the unit. Its `KillSignal=SIGINT` makes ffmpeg finish the last clip, which is listed before the recorder exits (`TimeoutStopSec=10`).
- **`stream.json`** in the stream folder (see `stream_meta.py`; every field optional): `live_id`, `preroll_started_at` (wall clock minus OBS's output duration at go-live), `go_live_pressed_at`, `live_confirmed_at`, `ended_at`, `resumed_at` (list), `mic_device`, `image` (`digest`, `version`, `reference` from `rpm-ostree status --json --booted`), `stream_pilot_version`, `fb_delay_s` (config, default 15), `replay_includes_preroll` (config, default `false`, measured in the phase 0 notes: the replay starts about 3 s after the Go Live press), `clock_at_live` / `clock_at_end` (`ntp_synchronized` from `timedatectl`, `offset_s` from `chronyc tracking`).
- **`DONE`** is written last in the end flow. A folder without it is still being written or was cut short.
- A recorder left running from an earlier stream (stream-pilot died while ending) is stopped at the next boot that does not resume a live, and before a new live's recorder starts.

## Behavior with the switch off

Today's behavior: no recorder, no `stream.json`, no `DONE`. The only thing that runs is the restore: a recorder unit still running from when the switch was on is stopped (at boot and on OBS connects).

## End-flow time budget (all features)

The power guard powers off 15 s after a press unless `status.json` says `ENDING`, then allows 90 s more. The end flow now has a budget, `END_FLOW_BUDGET_S = 70` s, from its start:

- stopping the stream (StopStream, waiting for OBS to stop, `fb_end_wait_s`) is never cut short;
- every later step (feature hooks, `capture.stop`) gets at most what is left of the budget minus 10 s kept for the tail, and is skipped when nothing is left;
- closing Chromium and stopping OBS get what is left, but at least 2 s each, then the power-off runs.

`test_recording.py` asserts that the constants fit the guard, and runs the whole end flow with every adapter and the recorder hung (scaled 1:100): it finishes in 70 s equivalent, under the asserted 75 s.

## Robustness

Every recording step is a feature hook: bounded, logged, never raised. A recorder that cannot start, a hung `systemctl`, or a broken `stream.json` never moves the controller to `ERROR`, never delays `READY`, and never delays go-live by more than the hook timeout (10 s; normally well under a second).

## Rollback

No new states; `state.json` is unchanged. `stream.json`, `DONE` and `audio/` are new files that an older image ignores. A recorder unit is not in older images, so after a rollback the switch has no effect.
